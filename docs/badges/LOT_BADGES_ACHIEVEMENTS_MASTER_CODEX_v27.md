## SECRET BOSS v15 — THE NEURAL VAULT

> *The deepest computing words. You have to know what they mean.*

| ID                | Symbol  | Trigger                                               | Rarity |
|-------------------|---------|-------------------------------------------------------|--------|
| `eniac_signal`    | ∘·∞     | Write "ENIAC" in any journal or memory entry          | rare   |
| `turing_complete` | ∞·∞     | Write "Turing complete" in any entry                  | epic   |
| `halting_problem` | ─○─     | Write "halting problem" in any entry                  | mythic |

**Lore**:
- `eniac_signal`: ENIAC (1945) — the first programmable general-purpose electronic computer. It filled a room, weighed 30 tons, and ran at 5,000 operations per second. Your phone is a billion times faster. ENIAC is where computation became a thing a machine could do.
- `turing_complete`: A system is Turing complete if it can compute anything computable. The concept that asks: what is the minimum required for universal computation? Writing this word means you know what the bar is.
- `halting_problem`: Turing's 1936 proof that no algorithm can determine, for all possible program-input pairs, whether the program will eventually halt or run forever. The first undecidable problem. The moment mathematics learned there are things it cannot know. *Sometimes you can't know when to stop either. That is wisdom, not failure.*

## THEMATIC NOTE — THE NEURAL ARCADE v17

The Neural Arcade marks a new evolution in LOT badge architecture:

**Previous engines** drew from:
- Physical practices (breathing, ocean, movement)
- Game mechanics (respawn, loot, dungeon)
- Cosmic mythology (alchemy, navigation, oracle)
- Sci-fi literature (quantum, cyberspace, singularity)

**v17 draws from**:
- Neuroscience as *self-care vocabulary* — the language that describes what actually happens when you take care of yourself, at the cellular and systems level
- Computing history as *origin myth* — ENIAC, the halting problem, the ARPANET crash that became "lo" are the creation stories of the digital world we live in
- The brain as *the ultimate gaming platform* — dopamine loops, habit stacks, flow states, and feedback signals are game mechanics that predate games

The word "neuroplasticity" entered mainstream vocabulary in the 1990s. The concept — that the brain changes shape based on experience — had been known since the 1890s (Cajal). We caught up slowly. LOT's job is to make the catching-up feel like play.
